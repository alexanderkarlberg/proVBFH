\section{The HH tensor matrix element}

\subsection{The two implementations}

The HH matrix element contracts the hadronic tensors of the two beams
with the $VV\to HH$ current,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma \propto W^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}\,W^{(2)}_{\rho\sigma}\,
  M^{\mu\rho}M^{*\nu\sigma},\qquad
  W_{\mu\nu} = F_1\,G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} + F_2\,G^{(2)}_{\mu\nu} + F_3\,G^{(3)}_{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
with the basis tensors
\begin{equation}
  G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^2} - g_{\mu\nu},\quad
  G^{(2)}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{\hat P_\mu \hat P_\nu}{P\cdot q},\quad
  G^{(3)}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{i\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}P^\rho q^\sigma}{2\,P\cdot q},\qquad
  \hat P = P - \frac{P\cdot q}{q^2}\,q,
\end{equation}
and the current
\begin{equation}
  M^{\mu\nu} = A\,g^{\mu\nu} + B\,(2k_1+q_1)^\mu(k_2-k_1-q_1)^\nu
             + C\,(2k_2+q_1)^\mu(k_1-k_2-q_1)^\nu ,
\end{equation}
where $A$ collects the triangle ($s$-channel Higgs), the quartic vertex
and the $g^{\mu\nu}$ part of the $t$/$u$-channel boxes, and $B$, $C$ the
remaining $t$/$u$-channel structures (with the coupling modifiers
$c_V$, $c_{VV}$, $\lambda$). The default \texttt{eval\_matrix\_element}
uses the analytic contraction (Mathematica expressions in
\file{ME\_expressions.f}); \texttt{eval\_matrix\_element\_tensor}
(\texttt{-tensorME}, \texttt{tensorME 1}) contracts numerically. The
tensor version is the only one with the diagram switches, and the
non-factorisable (NF) corrections always use it.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The F$_3$ bug was in the tensor version}{The F3 bug was in the tensor version}}

A first analysis (same-point comparison of the two implementations)
took the tensor version as the reference and concluded that the
$F_3\times F_3$ interference terms of the analytic \texttt{F3F3} were
wrong. \textbf{That was backwards.} The tensor code stored the
contravariant components of $q$, $P$ and $k_i$ in tensors declared with
lower indices, i.e.\ it worked with the parity-flipped vectors, but
built $\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}P^\rho q^\sigma$ from the unflipped
ones. Everything without $\epsilon$ is parity invariant and came out
right, as did $F_3\times F_3$ for $A$ alone; the $F_3\times F_3$
interference of $A$ with $B$, $C$ did not.

An independent calculation with explicit index handling at a physical
phase-space point (\file{scripts/f3-check/}) settles it: the analytic
\texttt{F3F3} coefficients (AA, AB, AC, BC) agree with it to all printed
digits, the old tensor convention only for AA (AB is off by a factor
39, AC by 4.4, BC by 22). Fixed in \commit{03adc62}: vectors are now
lowered with the metric (\texttt{InitFourVector} in \file{tensor.f90}).
The effect on the integrated HH cross section is at the $10^{-4}$ level
(analytic/old tensor $-1 = (-1.5\pm0.9)\cdot10^{-4}$).

\subsection{What remains: parity-odd width terms}

With finite widths the tensor version contains $F_1F_3$ and $F_2F_3$
terms, $\mathrm{Im}(AB^*)$ etc.\ times the $i\epsilon$ part of $W$,
which the analytic version does not have. Each has a single $\epsilon$
tensor, so they are parity odd and integrate to zero for $\sigma$ and
every parity-even distribution. Same-point comparisons after the fixes
(\file{scripts/dbg\_patch.py}, 0.6M points, HH at 14\,TeV):

\begin{center}
\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
comparison & analytic/tensor $-1$ (integral) & pointwise\\
\midrule
zero widths, LO           & $(-2\pm9)\cdot10^{-16}$ & $\le1.4\cdot10^{-5}$ (rare points)\\
zero widths, N$^3$LO      & $(+4\pm9)\cdot10^{-16}$ & $\le5.9\cdot10^{-6}$\\
physical widths, LO       & $(-2.2\pm1.2)\cdot10^{-4}$ & up to 46\% of the scale\\
widths, mirror-averaged tensor, LO & $+7\cdot10^{-16}$ & $\le1.4\cdot10^{-9}$ of the scale\\
same, N$^3$LO             & $-1\cdot10^{-15}$ & $\le3.9\cdot10^{-9}$ of the scale\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent (The scale is the sum of the absolute values of all terms;
``mirror-averaged'' means the tensor matrix element averaged over each
point and its mirror image $y\to-y$, which removes its parity-odd
part.)
So the analytic version is correct for all parity-even observables.
\textbf{Decision: the analytic matrix element stays the default}; the
tensor one is needed for parity-odd observables, the diagram switches
and the NF corrections.

\subsection{Order combination}

The analytic version expands strictly, $\sum_{n}\sum_{i+j=n+1}
F^{(i)}_{\text{beam 1}}F^{(j)}_{\text{beam 2}}$. The old tensor version
summed the structure functions over all orders first, which adds
products beyond the working order and ignores \texttt{order\_start}:
non-tensor/old tensor $-1$ without $F_3$ was $2\cdot10^{-15}$ (LO),
$+2.7\cdot10^{-4}$ (NLO), $+0.65\cdot10^{-4}$ (NNLO) and
$+0.06\cdot10^{-4}$ (N$^3$LO). Since $W$ is linear in the structure
functions, the new version (\commit{edc7a24}) computes once per point
and boson
\begin{equation}
  T_{kl} = \mathrm{Re}\left[G^{(k)}_{1,\mu\nu}\,G^{(l)}_{2,\rho\sigma}\,
  M^{\mu\rho}M^{*\nu\sigma}\right],\qquad
  \sigma \propto \sum_{n}\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{k,l}
  F_k^{(i)}F_l^{(j)}\,T_{kl},
\end{equation}
exactly the strict expansion, at no extra cost per order. With zero
widths, same-seed runs with and without \texttt{-tensorME} now give
identical results and MC errors at every order, also with coupling
modifiers (which the \provbfhinc tensor version ignored before
\commit{44d0d79}).

\subsection{Code changes and performance}

\begin{itemize}
\item \commit{8d00b5d}: \file{tensor.f90} rewritten without allocatable
  components (bit-identical results), the same file in all three
  packages. The previous version is kept as the oracle in
  \provbfhinc (\file{tests/}); \texttt{make check}
  runs a unit test against it and against explicit formulae
  (tolerances robust to fused multiply-adds since \commit{f706237}).
\item \commit{af68ff5}: \provbfhh used \texttt{cmplx()} without
  \texttt{kind=dp}, i.e.\ single-precision propagators
  ($1.5\cdot10^{-8}$ effect).
\item Timing of \texttt{provbfhh\_incl} (1M points, one core): old
  tensor 7.4\,s (LO) / 9.1\,s (N$^3$LO), new tensor 4.4 / 6.1\,s,
  analytic 2.7 / 4.4\,s.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Speed-up of the angular integrals}

Each phase-space point needs integrals over the gluon azimuth
$\xi\in[0,2\pi]$ of the eikonal integrands of
\file{nonfact\_expressions.f} (HH: 8 per boson, H: 2). They were done
with a Runge--Kutta routine with \texttt{niter}${}=100$ steps; since
the integrands do not depend on $y$, this is Simpson's rule with the
midpoint and the left endpoint evaluated twice, i.e.\ 400 calls per
integral, half of them redundant.

A study on 9000 argument sets dumped from real HH and H NF runs
(in \file{scripts/}) showed that the integrands are smooth
for typical kinematics, but at large transverse momenta develop peaks
of width $\sim\MV/p_T$ where $\xi$ aligns with an external transverse
momentum. No fixed rule copes (maximum error relative to $\int|f|$):

\begin{center}
\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
rule (calls) & $b_{01}$ & $b_{022}$ & $t_{022}$\\
\midrule
RK4, \texttt{niter} 100 (400) & $1.9\cdot10^{-4}$ & $8.1\cdot10^{-5}$ & $1.6\cdot10^{-1}$\\
trapezoidal (64) & $2.5\cdot10^{-3}$ & $1.1\cdot10^{-3}$ & $1.1$\\
Gauss--Legendre (128) & $4.0\cdot10^{-5}$ & $1.8\cdot10^{-5}$ & $8.5\cdot10^{-3}$\\
adaptive G--K, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ (mean calls) & $1.0\cdot10^{-11}$ (82) & $9\cdot10^{-10}$ (77) & $1.0\cdot10^{-9}$ (114)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent For RK4 on $t_{022}$ the median error is $2\cdot10^{-15}$,
the 99th percentile $2\cdot10^{-3}$. The changes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \commit{f37f0da}: the roots $r_1\ldots r_6$ were single-precision
  complex (\texttt{complex*8}; effect at most $1.6\cdot10^{-4}$ on the
  integrals).
\item \commit{d593bb4}: adaptive Gauss--Kronrod (G7/K15, 4 initial
  intervals, bisection of the worst interval) to a relative accuracy
  \texttt{nf\_epsrel} (new input, default $10^{-8}$) of $\int|f|$;
  \texttt{niter} is no longer used.
\item The integrands have the structure (with
  $L_k=\log(-r_k/\MV)$, $D_k=\pi r_k\prod_{j\ne k}(r_k-r_j)$)
  \begin{equation}
    b_{01}=-\sum_{k=1,3,5}\frac{L_k}{D_k},\quad
    b_{022}=-2\sum_{k=1,3,5}\frac{L_k^2}{D_k},\quad
    b_{12}=-2\,b_{01},
  \end{equation}
  and likewise $t_{01}$, $t_{022}$, $t_{12}=-2t_{01}$ for the triangle
  with $k=1,3$ and $j\le4$. \commit{03a4587} integrates the 1- and
  2-loop boxes together (shared roots, logs and peaks), and
  \commit{1a53e5e} folds the triangle into the integration of the first
  box of each boson (the box denominators are the triangle ones times
  $(r_k-r_5)(r_k-r_6)$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent Full HH NF run (notes statistics, 8 runs in parallel on 12
cores): $2332\,\mathrm{s}\to482\,\mathrm{s}$ with the adaptive
integration, $437\,\mathrm{s}$ with the folding, i.e.\ about $5\times$
faster. Every NF result (HH table, H) is identical to the old one in
every printed digit.

\paragraph{Impact on distributions.} The old integration was
inaccurate only at rare, extreme kinematics. Comparing all histograms
of the analysis bin by bin between the old and new runs (same seeds,
otherwise the same code; 35 histograms for HH, 14 for H), the largest
shift is 1.3\% in the $m_{HH}=3.2$--$3.4$\,TeV bin of the 1-loop HH
run, 5\% of its MC error; for the full HH NF correction 0.4\%
($p_{T,H}=780$--$810$\,GeV, 1.4\% of the MC error); for H
$2\cdot10^{-5}$. The paper's figures show NF/LO as a function of
$m_{jj}$ and $\Delta y_{jj}$ selection cuts: the cross sections above
an $m_{jj}$ or $\Delta y_{jj}$ cut change by at most $10^{-5}$ (full HH
NF), $1.4\cdot10^{-4}$ (1-loop HH, $m_{jj}>6.8$\,TeV) and
$2\cdot10^{-8}$ (H). The 2D cut grid of the figures was not rebuilt.

\subsection{Regulator independence}

The gluon-mass regulator was hard-wired to $\lambda=\MV^2$, where
$L\equiv\log(\lambda/\MV^2)=0$. With the identities above, the 1- and
2-loop functions have the form
\begin{equation}
  \chi^{(1)}_a = f_a - L,\qquad \chi^{(2)}_a = C_a - 2f_aL + L^2,
\end{equation}
for each diagram class $a$, with $M_1=i\sum_a\chi^{(1)}_aX_a$ and
$M_2=-\frac12\sum_a\chi^{(2)}_aX_a$, so that the $L$ terms cancel point
by point in $|M_1|^2+2\,\mathrm{Re}(M_2M_0^*)$. \commit{fe67abd}
therefore obtains $b_{12}$, $t_{12}$ from the 1-loop integrals and adds
the input \texttt{nf\_regfact} ($\lambda=$\,\texttt{nf\_regfact}$\cdot\MV^2$,
default 1). The 1-loop triangle is computed analytically
(\texttt{chi\_tri1}); the numerical $-2\int t_{01}/t_{11}$ agrees with it
to $1.7\cdot10^{-11}$, so $\lambda$ independence also tests the
angular integration. Same-seed runs with \texttt{nf\_regfact}
$0.01$, $1$ and $100$ give identical results in all printed digits (HH
all diagrams, HH triangles only, H).

\section{Continuous integration}

\file{.github/workflows/ci.yml} builds the three packages on
\texttt{ubuntu-latest} and \texttt{macos-latest} against the latest
releases of \hoppet, LHAPDF and FastJet (on push/PR, weekly, on
demand), with the logic in \file{ci/*.sh} so that it runs locally too:
\begin{itemize}
\item \file{smoke-tests.sh}: short runs of every program, checking for
  complete and finite output (not a physics validation);
\item \file{tensor-checks.sh}: \texttt{make check}, identical copies of
  \file{tensor.f90}, and tensor vs analytic matrix element at zero
  widths (LO and NNLO);
\item \file{nf-checks.sh}: NF runs of \provbfh and \provbfhh with
  $\lambda=\MV^2$ and $0.01\,\MV^2$, which must agree to $10^{-6}$ (a
  deliberately wrong sign in $b_{12}=-2b_{01}$ changes the result by a
  factor 100);
\item \file{annotate.sh}: reports the error lines of a failing step as
  annotations in the run summary.
\end{itemize}
The first macOS runs exposed: the unit test demanded bit-identical
rounding (fails with FMA on arm64); the tensor module stopped with exit
status 0 on errors (now \texttt{error stop}); the Fortran
module-directory flag reached clang++; \texttt{-mcmodel=large} is not
supported there (\commit{f706237}, \commit{b70d3af}). CI is green on
both platforms. The dependency cache is saved right after installing
(\commit{b8f1eb2}, \commit{91f8bbb}; the saved caches, 25\,MB on macOS
and 39\,MB on Linux, are now in place). Also fixed on the way:
\texttt{make -j} failed (\commit{44e051a}).

\section{Open points}
\begin{itemize}
\item HH Born normalisation vs 2005.11334 (PDF not stated in the paper):
  accepted as is.
\item The 2018 HH papers used the old tensor code: their numbers
  include the $F_3$ error and the beyond-accuracy order products, both
  at the $10^{-4}$ level, far below the quoted uncertainties.
\item Possible further NF speed-up: initial Gauss--Kronrod break
  points at the known peak angles.
\item \texttt{incl2pwhg.f} ignores \texttt{tensorME} (consistent with
  keeping the analytic default).
\item Merging \texttt{ci} into \texttt{main}.
\end{itemize}
